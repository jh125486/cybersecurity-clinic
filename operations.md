# Clinic operations guide

## Set up a pilot

- Name a club coordinator and a faculty or professional advisor. Assign each engagement a team lead, partner liaison, evidence lead, and reporting lead. One volunteer can cover two roles. Keep an alternate liaison and reviewer available.
- Start with one small organization that has a named decision-maker, time for a kickoff and closeout, and a business question the team can answer within a semester. Examples include reducing account lockouts, checking whether critical backups can be restored, or clarifying how staff report suspicious email.
- Ask about critical dates, vendor responsibilities, interruptions the organization cannot tolerate, and any work already underway. Refer active incidents to the organization's designated responder or IT provider.
- Schedule training, a scope review, a site visit, weekly check-ins, a draft review, and a client briefing. Agree on a private location for records and designate who can access it.

## Move an engagement through five checkpoints

| Checkpoint | Team responsibility | Ready to proceed when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intake | Record the request, business outcome, contact, staff availability, and exclusions | The client decision-maker and advisor agree the project fits the clinic |
| Scope | Confirm included systems, agreed review methods, evidence handling, contacts, dates, and expected outputs in writing | The partner and advisor approve the scope |
| Evidence review | Interview staff, document observations, check partner-supplied artifacts, and record evidence gaps | The advisor can trace proposed findings to evidence or clearly marked assumptions |
| Release | Prioritize three to five actions and draft the memorandum and briefing | The advisor approves the materials and the liaison confirms who will receive them |
| Closeout | Deliver the report, agree on owners and timing, and handle working records per the agreed retention date | The partner acknowledges receipt and the coordinator records the disposition of the files |

Use the [intake and scope worksheet](templates.md#partner-intake-and-scope) and [weekly check-in](templates.md#weekly-check-in) to track decisions.

## Run the partner visit

1. Reconfirm scope, contact, time, and whether note-taking or any photographs have been agreed. Assign a facilitator, note-taker, and timekeeper.
2. Ask the partner to walk through one essential business process in plain language. Identify which people, devices, accounts, providers, and information it depends on.
3. Review only the evidence needed for the agreed questions. Ask a client representative to demonstrate relevant settings or provide redacted summaries. Log the source, date, observation, and any limitations.
4. End by restating key observations and open questions. Agree on who can clarify disputed or incomplete information and when the team will report back.

## Review foundational practices

| Area | Questions and evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Governance and contacts | Who decides on IT changes, who supports the service, and how does staff reach them? Ask for a current role list or contact procedure. |
| Assets and vendors | Which systems keep the business operating, who owns them, and which vendor dependencies could interrupt work? Compare partner-provided records to the walkthrough. |
| Identity and access | Who approves administrator access, how does staff offboarding work, and where is MFA in use? Review a redacted role or account summary with the owner. |
| Devices and software | Who handles updates, where are managed devices recorded, and what happens when one is lost? Ask for a summary of update and management coverage. |
| Data and backups | Which data needs to be recoverable, who owns backups, and when did the partner last verify a restore? Ask for the outcome of the last client-managed restore or record the evidence gap. |
| Communications | How does staff report suspicious messages, approve payment changes, and share sensitive files? Walk through a fictional incident with the organization. |
| Incident readiness | Who coordinates a suspected incident, where are response contacts kept, and which decisions require leadership? Ask staff to explain the first few actions in a tabletop. |

Use the [NIST small-business guide](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/1300/final) to structure conversations about governing, identifying, protecting, detecting, responding, and recovering.

## Handle evidence and findings

Record only the minimum evidence needed. Use partner-approved communication and restricted storage, and keep working files out of public repositories and club chat. Record findings with a business impact, supporting evidence, confidence, recommended action, owner, and timing. Label what came from an interview, what the team observed, and what remains unverified. Keep the client report clear about scope and limitations.

If something unexpected occurs, pause the activity, contact the advisor and partner point of contact through the agreed channel, and document the decision and time. Leave any proposed changes to client systems with the responsible client owner or provider, unless explicitly agreed and supervised. At closeout, follow the agreed transfer and retention plan and remove access that the team no longer needs.
