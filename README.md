# Cybersecurity Clinic

A handbook for student cybersecurity clubs that want to run a supervised, pro bono clinic for small businesses, nonprofits, or public-sector organizations with limited IT support. The clinic helps partners understand their current security practices and leave with a short, practical improvement plan.

## Start here

1. **Club officers and advisors** — use the [operations guide](operations.md) to staff the clinic, select a pilot partner, agree on scope, and schedule review checkpoints.
2. **Facilitators and volunteers** — work through the [seven teaching modules](curriculum.md) before meeting with a partner. Use fictional cases for practice.
3. **Engagement teams** — use the [worksheets and deliverable templates](templates.md) from intake through the client memorandum and closeout.

## Engagement at a glance

| Stage | What the team does | What the advisor reviews |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prepare | Learn the clinic workflow, practice on a fictional case, and acknowledge the code of conduct | Volunteer readiness and assigned roles |
| Agree on scope | Confirm the business question, point of contact, review methods, data handling, and deliverables | Written scope before fieldwork starts |
| Assess | Interview staff, review partner-provided evidence, build an inventory, and identify risks | Evidence quality, limitations, and proposed findings |
| Deliver | Write the client memorandum, brief leadership, and agree on owners and priorities | Client-facing materials before release |
| Close | Hand over the approved files and follow the agreed retention plan | Closeout record and lessons for the next cohort |

A pilot can start with one partner and a team of four or five students. The UNT model includes a two- to three-hour site visit and a 30-minute nontechnical briefing. Adjust the schedule and scope to the partner's capacity.

The workflows and fill-in templates are editable Markdown adaptations, and readings point to their publishers or public reading lists.
